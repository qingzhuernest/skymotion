Why does Earth rise and set?

A lunar orbiter changes its observing position. The Moon reveals or hides Earth as the line of sight crosses the lunar limb.

Model assumptions and scale

Independent teaching model: lunar radius 1,737.4 km and Earth–Moon distance 384,400 km, with circular orbits and synchronous lunar rotation. Sun–Earth–Moon geometry is held fixed during each orbital demonstration. The fixed surface site is at the center of the near side.

The astronaut view uses physical geometry and an adjustable field of view; the diagram scales bodies and orbits separately. Surface textures are illustrative. Terrain, libration, orbital inclination, eclipses and atmospheric refraction are omitted. This is not a tool for mission planning or date-based prediction.
