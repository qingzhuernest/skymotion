Scale and model notes

Positions come from Astronomy Engine; orbit guides sample the ephemeris. Heliocentric distances use a power-law display compression, while body sizes are enlarged separately. The Sun–Earth–Moon view uses a separate Earth–Moon distance scale. Screen sizes and distances are not to physical scale.

In the Sun–Earth–Moon view, the arrow points toward the distant Sun and sunlight is parallel. The Moon seen from the external camera is not the phase seen from Earth; its geocentric illuminated fraction is calculated separately. Surface and cloud textures are illustrative, not images for the selected date. Earth's rotation and axis follow UTC; other bodies' orientations are illustrative. Eclipses and local rise/set events are not simulated here.

The date slider changes UTC continuously; selecting a calendar date sets 12:00 UTC. Rotation and zoom change only the camera, not physical positions. This page has its own state, so your main lab settings are preserved.

Reference views share the same UTC. The top view projects onto the fixed ecliptic plane; the local sky shows azimuth and altitude at the selected location. These 2D views stay fixed as the 3D camera rotates, distinguishing spatial motion from apparent sky motion.
